DEEP CLEAN VS STANDARD CLEAN: WHICH ONE DO YOU NEED?

Booking the wrong one is the most common mistake people make. Here is how to tell them apart and choose correctly the first time.

Get an Instant Quote

The difference in one sentence

A standard clean maintains a home that is already at a reasonable baseline. A deep clean establishes that baseline in the first place.

That distinction is the whole thing. Standard cleaning covers the surfaces you see and touch every day — counters, bathrooms, floors, dusting. Deep cleaning covers those plus the accumulated areas routine cleaning never reaches: inside the oven, inside the refrigerator, baseboards, vent covers, window sills and tracks, cabinet fronts and interiors, grout detail.

A simple test

Run a finger along the top edge of a baseboard in a room you don't use much. If it comes back visibly grey, the home is past standard-clean territory.

Check the window tracks in a bedroom, and the vent cover in the bathroom. Those three spots tell you more about a home's actual condition than the kitchen counter does, because nobody cleans them incidentally.

The other reliable signal is time. If it has been more than about three months since a thorough clean, book the deep clean. Almost nobody regrets that decision; plenty of people regret the reverse.

Why the first visit is usually a deep clean

Most reputable companies recommend starting with a deep clean, and it is not an upsell. A standard clean is scoped and timed against a known baseline. When a cleaner arrives at a home that has drifted well past that, they face a choice between rushing everything or doing part of the house properly and leaving the rest.

Establishing the baseline once means every subsequent visit is predictable, faster and cheaper. After the initial deep clean, most homes hold comfortably on a biweekly standard schedule.

When a standard clean is the right call

If the home is already on a regular cleaning schedule and you are simply switching providers, a standard clean is usually fine — the baseline exists, it just needs maintaining.

If you clean thoroughly yourself and want help maintaining rather than resetting, standard is also correct. And if you are between deep cleans on a seasonal rhythm — many households do a deep clean in spring and autumn with standard cleans in between — that pattern works well.

Older homes are a special case

Indianapolis has a lot of genuinely old housing stock — the streets around the Greenfield courthouse square, the Village in Zionsville, downtown Noblesville. Original tile, older grout and detailed millwork hold grime in ways modern finishes don't.

In those homes a deep clean is effectively mandatory as a first visit, and periodic deep cleans stay worthwhile even on a regular schedule, because the surfaces themselves reclaim buildup faster.
