HOW OFTEN SHOULD YOU HAVE YOUR HOUSE CLEANED?

Most people default to a schedule that doesn't match their household. Here is how to pick the right frequency and what happens when you stretch it too far.

Get an Instant Quote

Biweekly is the right default for most homes

For the majority of households, every two weeks is the point where the home stays consistently presentable without paying for work that isn't needed yet. It is comfortably the most common schedule we run, and it suits a typical family home with normal use.

Two weeks is roughly how long it takes a maintained home to drift far enough that the difference is visible but not far enough that the next visit becomes a reset. That is the economically efficient point.

Go weekly if any of these apply

Multiple pets, particularly shedding breeds. Pet hair does not accumulate gradually — it reaches a visible threshold quickly and stays there.

Young children at home during the day, or anyone in the household with significant allergies or asthma, where dust load genuinely affects wellbeing.

Large households, or homes where people work from home full-time. Occupancy hours drive soiling far more than square footage does.

Monthly works for a narrower set of homes

Monthly suits smaller households, single occupants, part-time residences and homes where the occupants clean regularly themselves and want periodic thorough help rather than maintenance.

It also fits larger rural properties where the practical rhythm is different — a monthly or six-week cycle with more time per visit often serves those homes better than a tighter schedule.

What happens when you stretch it

Past about six weeks without a clean, most homes stop being a maintenance job. Grout reclaims build-up, bathroom surfaces need a deeper approach, and hard-floor edges accumulate to the point of needing hand-work.

The result is that a stretched schedule doesn't actually save money — it converts cheaper maintenance visits into more expensive reset visits. If budget is the constraint, a smaller scope at a regular frequency almost always beats a full clean at a stretched one.

Match the schedule to the flooring

Homes that are mostly hard-surface flooring show dust and footmarks faster but clean faster too, which favours more frequent, shorter visits.

Carpet-heavy homes hide day-to-day soiling but hold it, which favours a slightly longer interval with more thorough vacuuming. A lot of newer Hamilton and Boone County construction is hard-surface on the main level and carpet upstairs, which is why biweekly suits it so well.
